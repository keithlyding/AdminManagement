# The daily routine

The lists and flows bring things into one place; the routine keeps that place true. This is
an opinion, not a rule: a common way to make one list stick. Change it to fit how you work.

## The one rule

**If it's an action item, it goes in Action Items.** Not in a flagged email you'll come back
to, not only in OneNote, not in your head. Everything else (email, OneNote, L2L) is where
work arrives; the list is where it's tracked.

## Morning (5 minutes)

1. **To Sort**: give every new item a Category and Necessity (and a Due Date if it has one).
   Delete what isn't really yours or isn't an action. Empty this view every day.
2. **Due in 7 Days or Past Due**: anything red gets done, rescheduled or handed off today.
3. **My Open Tasks**: the top 3 are today's plan.

## During the day

- An email asks you for something: **flag it**. Flow 1 does the rest.
- A meeting produces an action: add it, or mark it in OneNote for later.
- An L2L dispatch is assigned to you: until the L2L link exists, add it with Source = L2L
  and the dispatch number in the Description.

## End of day (10 minutes)

1. The **Process meeting notes** item: go through today's OneNote pages, move each action
   in, mark the item Complete.
2. Update Status and the Notes column on what moved today.
3. Unflag emails whose items are in the list (optional, keeps the flag list short).

## Weekly (15 minutes, Friday)

- **Others' Open Tasks**: chase what you're waiting on.
- **Recurring Work**: check the Next Due of Manual duties.
- **Completed**: a look back, useful for status updates.
